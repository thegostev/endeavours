Pomorskie field guide

20 interesting places to visit from Gdańsk Oliwa

A curated collection of unusual architecture, archaeological sites, coastal landscapes, engineering landmarks and regional museums, using Zamek Kazimierczaka w Łapalicach as the starting point for the selection. The emphasis is on places with a distinctive story or atmosphere, rather than a comprehensive list of the region’s best-known sights.

All journeys start at Opacka 20, Gdańsk and are one-way.

Distances and driving times are Google Maps route snapshots checked on 15 November 2026, not straight-line distances or guaranteed journey times.

    Full story and sources: Torpedownia Babie Doły, Gdynia
    • Why it is significant: The offshore structure is the surviving ruin of a German Second World War torpedo facility (Pomorskie.travel). Its position a few hundred metres out to sea makes wartime infrastructure an unusually conspicuous feature of the present coastline (Pomorskie.travel).
    • What is special: The combination of a sandy beach and a large, isolated concrete ruin offers a very different coastal scene from a conventional seaside promenade (Pomorskie.travel).     Full story and sources: Zamek Kazimierczaka w Łapalicach
    • Why it is significant: Piotr Kazimierczak’s project began in 1983 with permission for a house and sculpture studio, but grew into an unfinished castle-like building of about 5,000 m² (Rzeczpospolita).     Kamienne kręgi w Węsiorach

    Stone circles and burial mounds from the 1st-3rd centuries CE.

    • Archaeology
    • Coast and nature
    By car
    76.3 km / 1 h 13 min
    Rail-based Full journey, verified
    Total: 3 h, Mon; tram + train + bus 7 + walking
    Route ends at
    Road approach to the archaeological site; forest exploration additional.

    Driving route to Kamienne kręgi w Węsiorach → Transit route to Kamienne kręgi w Węsiorach →

    Full story and sources: Kamienne kręgi w Węsiorach
    • Why it is significant: This burial ground is associated with the Wielbark culture and the Goths/Gepids, and dates broadly to the first–third centuries CE (Pomorskie.travel). The site preserves three stone circles, fragments of a fourth and twenty burial mounds, making an ancient funerary landscape visible without needing to imagine everything from an excavation plan (Pomorskie.travel).
    • What is special: The largest circle is around 26 metres across, with upright stones and additional stone pillars inside it (Pomorskie.travel). The appeal is the quiet encounter with real archaeological remains; claims about mysterious “energy” should be treated as folklore, not the reason the site matters.
    • Visit note: This is a good choice if you prefer atmosphere and a modest walk to large buildings or extensive museum interpretation. Treat the stones and burial features as archaeological remains, not climbing equipment.

    A brick railway bridge from about 1884, styled like a Roman aqueduct.

    • Engineering

    Rail-based Partial journey only

    Full story and sources: Most kolejowy nad Borują, Bytów
    • Why it is significant: The historic railway bridge dates from around 1884, with six brick-vaulted spans resting on granite masonry supports (Bytów municipality).     • Visit note: The restored bridge officially reopened on 30 April 2019 (Bytów municipality). It is a natural architectural companion to Bytów Castle, though the travel table gives separate journeys from Gdańsk rather than an inter-stop route.

    04 Forest archaeology and the Słowiński coast

    Kamienne kręgi w Odrach

    Ten stone circles in pine forest above the Wda river.

    • Archaeology
    • Coast and nature
    By car
    119 km / 1 h 47 min
    Rail-based Partial journey only
    Total: n.a. Czersk station leg: 2 h 09 min, Sun; trains + walking, then an unverified onward leg.
    Route ends at
    Archaeological reserve; car route has tolls.

    Driving route to Kamienne kręgi w Odrach → Partial transit leg towards Kamienne kręgi w Odrach →

    Full story and sources: Kamienne kręgi w Odrach
    • Why it is significant: The cemetery was founded around 70–80 CE and used into the early third century by people of the Wielbark culture, associated with the Goths (Chojnice museum). The museum records ten stone circles, thirty burial mounds and hundreds of graves, giving the site an unusually extensive surviving funerary landscape (Chojnice museum).

The stones also support an important lichen community, including rare species, which adds a second layer of natural-history interest (Chojnice museum).
    • Visit note: A separate museum exhibition in the village explains the Goths and objects excavated from the cemetery (Chojnice museum). Check access arrangements for both the reserve and the exhibition rather than assuming they share a schedule.

    • Why it is significant: The museum opened in 1963 to preserve the material culture of the Słowińcy and later developed into a reconstruction of historic Kluki’s village layout (museum history). Several farmsteads remain in their original positions, giving the site an authenticity beyond a collection of buildings moved into an unrelated park (museum buildings).
    • What is special: Timber-framed, reed-thatched cottages, farm buildings, wells and bread ovens create a coherent historic village environment (museum buildings). Furnished interiors and displays about fishing and domestic life make it especially appealing if you enjoy everyday history rather than primarily military or aristocratic history (museum history).
    • Visit note: Check the current museum programme and opening arrangements before travelling (museum website). The verified rail-and-bus example takes 5 h 25 min one-way, so it should not be mistaken for a convenient train day trip from Oliwa (Google Maps itinerary).
