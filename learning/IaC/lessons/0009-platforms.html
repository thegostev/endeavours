Where you are

The big idea

Platform - the one thing
Self-service delivery and infrastructure for app teams - instead of every team building its own.
Golden path - the one thing
The supported, easiest way to do a common job - instead of each team finding its own.
In a paragraph
The CNCF platforms group describes platforms as curating and presenting common capabilities to internal users, and platform engineering as planning and providing them - people, processes, policies and technology. In this course's words: a platform team builds the road once (pipelines, IaC modules, runtime, guardrails) and offers it as self-service. App teams follow golden paths: ready, supported ways to do common jobs, such as "a new web service" or "a new database".
Picture
A city's roads and bus lines. The city builds them once, signs them and keeps them safe. You can walk your own way through a field - but the road is faster, lit, and someone fixes it when it breaks.
Where the picture stops working
City roads are free to use and nobody asks if you like them. An internal platform has customers who can choose not to use it, and it is built for them. That is why platform engineering treats the platform as a product, with product managers (the CNCF maturity model names the role).

Words you need

platform
Shared capabilities - pipelines, IaC modules, runtime, guardrails - offered to app teams as self-service. Also called an internal developer platform.
platform engineering
The practice of planning and providing platforms: the people, processes, policies and technology.
self-service
A team gets what it needs (a database, a new service) by itself, through a template or a portal, without a ticket and a wait.
golden path
The opinionated and supported way to do a common job (Spotify's term; Netflix says "paved road"). Teams may leave it, but then they own the extra work.
guardrails
Rules the platform enforces behind the self-service: policy as code, IAM, cost limits.
developer portal
The front door of a platform: a catalog and forms (Backstage is one). Not the whole platform.
platform as a product
Running the platform for its users - the app teams - with research, a roadmap and measures of success.
observability
Knowing what a running system does from outside, through logs (events as text), metrics (numbers over time) and traces (one request's path).

The platform, as one picture

Five moments with app teams

Moment 1 - a database in two weeks

The Search team needs a database. Today they open a ticket. The platform team writes the definition files by hand, reviews and applies them. It takes two weeks.

Reveal

The Search team picks "new database" in a template, fills in a size and a name, and gets it in an hour. Behind the form, nothing new: the same IaC module (0007), the same pipeline with a plan and policy checks (0008), the same IAM rules.

Why: self-service does not remove the checks - it removes the waiting. The platform team builds the road once, and every request takes it. Their work moves from doing requests to improving the road.

Moment 2 - off the path

The Payments team wants a different database engine that the golden path does not offer.

Reveal

No. They may leave it. Then they own the extra work: their own module, their own runbooks, no support from the platform team.

Why: Spotify describes golden paths as the "opinionated and supported" way, and still lets teams stray when they need to. A golden path wins by being the easiest road, not by being the only road. If many teams leave it for the same reason, that is a signal for the platform's roadmap.

Moment 3 - "we bought a portal"

A director says: "We set up Backstage last quarter, so now we have a platform."

Reveal

A portal is the front door. The platform is what the door leads to: golden paths, IaC modules, pipelines, runtime, guardrails. A portal with nothing reliable behind it is a nice catalog of tickets.

Why: the CNCF glossary lists many names for platforms, and a portal is one way to present one. Value comes from the capabilities behind it. Look at the platform figure: the front door is one row of five.

Moment 4 - nobody uses the new feature

The platform team spent a quarter building automatic database cloning. Two teams use it. Most teams still copy data by hand.

Reveal

Talked to the app teams first: what slows them down, which job they do most often, what they would stop doing if the platform did it. Then built the smallest thing that removed that pain, and measured whether teams used it.

Why: the CNCF maturity model treats the platform as a product with internal customers, and names platform product managers. A platform nobody chooses has failed, however good its engineering is.

Moment 5 - an AI front door

Someone proposes to add an AI chat (like Spacelift Intent) to the portal: "just ask for a database".

No answer here on purpose. Your answer, and your reason. Think about lesson 0005: what a reviewed definition file gives, and where Spacelift itself places Intent. Then think about your own HwtP for Infra users. Write 3-4 sentences in chat.

Say the difference - the same words in every lesson

Infrastructure vs platform vs environment (settled)

Infrastructure is the floor. A platform is the road on top of it, offered as self-service. An environment is one use of the floor: infrastructure plus config, for one purpose.

The platform makes environments on the floor for teams.

Portal vs platform

The portal is the front door. The platform is what the door leads to.

Backstage is a portal. The golden paths behind it are the platform.

Golden path vs the only path

A golden path is the easiest supported road. Teams may leave it and own the extra work.

If many leave it for one reason, change the path.

Self-service vs no checks

Self-service removes the waiting, not the review.

The same module, plan and policy run behind every request.

The same module, plan, policy and IAM - only the waiting is gone.

How do you know if a platform feature works?

App teams choose to use it, and it saves them time. Measure use, not shipping.

Where does IaC sit in a platform?

Under the golden paths: modules and an infrastructure pipeline that every self-service request goes through.
